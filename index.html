M.Sc. Dissertation May 2026 TNBC PARP Inhibitors Python Automation

Role of Compensatory SUMOylation Mechanisms in NSMCE2-Deficient Breast Cancer Cells

Department of Molecular Genetics and Cancer, NUCSER, Nitte (Deemed to be University) · Guided by Dr. Vivek Tripathi & Dr. Sudarshan Kini

Read Full Thesis (PDF)
Biological Question
Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC) suffers from severe replication stress and genomic instability, yet develops marked resistance to DNA-damaging therapies like PARP inhibitors (Olaparib). Does NSMCE2 (the SUMO E3 ligase in the SMC5/6 chromosomal maintenance complex) coordinate an adaptive survival response that protects stalled replication forks and confers chemoresistance?
Hypothesis & Rationale
We hypothesized that elevated NSMCE2 expression provides an adaptive mechanism allowing TNBC cells to survive chronic replication stress. When perturbed, cells may leverage compensatory SUMOylation nodes (e.g. UBC9, PIAS1/4) to maintain chromatin stability, avoid fork collapse, and sustain cellular migration under chemotherapeutic pressure.
Experimental Approach
Compared two TNBC lines (mesenchymal MDA-MB-231 and basal-like MDA-MB-468) against luminal ER+ control (MCF-7). Conducted RT-qPCR (18S rRNA & GAPDH reference genes), resolved protein quantification discrepancies by switching from Bradford to detergent-compatible BCA assay, performed Western blotting (anti-NSMCE2 ~28–29 kDa vs β-actin ~40 kDa), colchicine metaphase karyotyping, and wound-scratch migration assays under Olaparib (1000 nM).
Verified Key Findings
1. Upregulation: NSMCE2 mRNA and protein levels were significantly higher in both TNBC lines compared to MCF-7 (52.7-fold in MDA-MB-468, 21.6-fold in MDA-MB-231 vs GAPDH).
2. Migration Resilience: While Olaparib inhibited migration across all cell lines, MDA-MB-231 exhibited partial resistance to anti-migratory effects (87–89% closure at 24h).
3. Hyper-triploidy: Metaphase spreads demonstrated hyper-triploid chromosome distributions (>60 chromosomes).

B.Sc. Dissertation May 2024 Nanomedicine Molecular Docking AutoDock Vina FESEM

Rutin-Encapsulated PEG-PLGA Nanoparticles for Modulating Autophagy and Metabolism in Cancer

SynTech Bio Solutions Lab, Dayananda Sagar University · Guided by Dr. Pronama Biswas

Read Full Thesis (PDF)
Biological Question
Rutin is a natural dietary flavonoid with known anti-tumor and antioxidant activities, but its therapeutic utility is severely limited by poor aqueous solubility and low bioavailability. Can encapsulation into biodegradable PEG-PLGA nanoparticles improve stability, and how effectively does Rutin bind key regulators of autophagy and metabolic reprogramming compared to FDA-approved inhibitors?
Formulation Approach
Employed Solvent-Antisolvent Nanoprecipitation. Rutin and PEG-PLGA (MW 55 kDa) were dissolved in organic solvents (DMSO/DCM) and introduced drop-wise via syringe pump into an aqueous anti-solvent phase stabilized by Polyvinyl Alcohol (PVA 30–70 kDa). Optimized stirring speed, solvent ratios, and centrifugation across four experimental formulation trials.
Characterization & FESEM
Surface morphology and particle size distribution were analyzed via Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy (FESEM) using JSM-7100F and Apreo 2 SEM with gold/chromium sputtering. Trial 2 successfully generated uniform spherical nanoparticles with distinct porous surface morphology.
Computational Validation
Retrieved crystal structures from RCSB PDB, validated stereochemistry using PROCHECK Ramachandran plots (>93% residues in favored regions), VERIFY3D, and VoroMQA. Performed molecular docking with AutoDock Vina, Discovery Studio Visualizer, and UCSF Chimera across 6 critical cancer targets.

Scientific Mindset

How I Think and Work as a Scientist

Core operating principles that guide my experiments, problem-solving habits, and research aspirations.

01

I Build Bridges Between Disciplines

I am trained where chemistry, biochemistry, and genetics converge. Having worked at the bench with mammalian cells, designed molecular delivery vehicles, modeled docking in silico, and observed sarcoma patient care at a major cancer centre, I am comfortable operating across the lab bench, computational script, and clinic.

02

I Make My Own Tools

When a laboratory protocol is slow, subjective, or bottlenecked, I don't settle for the manual friction. Developing an automated Python image segmentation pipeline for wound-scratch assays taught me that an experimental biologist who can code is empowered to ask deeper questions and process larger datasets with reproducibility.

03

I Keep Patients Firmly in View

During my internship at HCG Cancer Centre reviewing decades of sarcoma survivorship records, I witnessed how oncologist teams debate patient trajectories. A molecular question matters far more when you can picture the patient whose therapeutic resistance it might one day overcome.

04

I Explain Things from First Principles

Teaching ICSE chemistry to adolescents has made me patient, rigorous, and intellectually honest. If you cannot explain the molecular rationale of a reaction or a therapeutic mechanism in simple terms, you haven't mastered it yet.
